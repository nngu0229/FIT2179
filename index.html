How Immigration Powers Australia's Workforce and Economy

1. The Big Picture

Australia's economy has been strongly shaped by immigration, as migrants bring skills, labour, and cultural diversity into the workforce. This visualisation explores how immigration contributes to economic growth by showing where migrants settle, which industries they support, and how they help address labour demand. Rather than viewing immigration only as population growth, this project presents it as an important force behind Australia's workforce and long-term economic development.

Australia's Overseas-Born Population Continues to Rise

Australia's population growth has been closely tied to net overseas migration rather than natural population increase alone. While natural increase remained relatively stable over time, migration became the largest contributor to total population growth, particularly after the reopening of international borders following COVID-19.

Where Migrants Settle in Australia

Migrants are not evenly distributed across Australia. States such as Western Australia, Victoria, and New South Wales consistently show higher overseas-born population shares, reflecting their stronger links to jobs, universities, and urban economic centres. The slider shows how migrant settlement has grown across most states from 2001 to 2021.

Top Migrant-Origin Country in Australia

Australia's migrant population comes from a wide range of countries, reflecting the nation's multicultural identity and global migration links. Countries such as England, India, China, and New Zealand represent some of the largest overseas-born communities, contributing significantly to Australia's workforce, education sector, and economic growth.

Behind Australia's economic growth is a workforce quietly holding industries together. Migrants make up a significant share of workers across nearly every sector — from hospitals and construction sites to tech offices and restaurant kitchens. But their contribution isn't spread evenly. Some industries lean heavily on overseas-born workers to fill roles that not enough Australians are taking up. This section maps where migrants are working across Australia — by industry and by state — revealing the invisible backbone of the Australian labour market.
