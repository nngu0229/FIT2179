The Big Picture

Australia's recent population growth has been driven overwhelmingly by migration rather than natural increase. As fertility rates decline and population ageing accelerates, net overseas migration (NOM) has become a critical source of workforce expansion, economic activity, and long-term demographic sustainability.

This section introduces the national migration landscape by examining where new migrants settle across Australia. Rather than dispersing evenly across the country, migration is heavily concentrated in a small number of metropolitan centres where labour demand, educational opportunities, and economic activity are strongest.

Migration Density

Mapping Regional Migration Distribution

The benefits of migration are not distributed uniformly across Australia. New arrivals overwhelmingly settle in major urban centres, creating dense migration clusters around Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Canberra. These locations act as economic gateways, attracting skilled workers, international students, and permanent migrants who support local labour markets and population growth. Population Growth

Australia's Population Growth Is Increasingly Driven by Migration

For most of the past decade, Australia's population growth has been supported by two sources: natural increase (births minus deaths) and net overseas migration (NOM). However, as fertility rates declined and population ageing accelerated, migration gradually became the dominant contributor to national population growth.

The disruption caused by COVID-19 border closures temporarily reversed this trend, causing migration to collapse and population growth to slow sharply. Following the reopening of international borders in late 2022, migration rebounded rapidly, becoming the primary force behind Australia's population recovery and workforce expansion.

Data Source: ABS National, State and Territory Population (Sep 2025)
Country of Origin

Australia's Migrant Origins Have Shifted Towards Asia

Australia's overseas-born population has undergone a significant transformation over the past decade. While migration was historically dominated by arrivals from the United Kingdom and other European countries, recent growth has been increasingly driven by Asian nations. This shift reflects Australia's changing economic ties, expanding international education sector, and growing demand for skilled workers from rapidly developing economies.

Filling Australia's Skill Gaps

Australia's workforce challenges extend beyond labour shortages to shortages of specialised skills. In many industries, domestic supply alone is insufficient to meet growing demand for qualified workers. Skilled migration helps bridge these gaps by providing expertise in occupations that are critical to economic productivity and long-term growth. This section explores which industries rely most heavily on migrant skills and how skilled migration contributes to Australia's economic output.

05

Myth vs Reality

Economic contribution is often easier to measure than public perception. While previous sections demonstrated how migrants support population growth, fill workforce shortages, and contribute to economic output, concerns persist that immigration may come at a cost to local workers. One of the most common arguments in public debate is that migrants increase competition for jobs and place downward pressure on wages. If this claim is true, we would expect migrants to experience substantially different labour market outcomes compared with Australian-born residents, particularly in employment rates and earnings. This final chart compares unemployment rates and median weekly earnings between Australian-born and overseas-born Australians. By examining actual labour market outcomes rather than public perception, we can assess whether migration appears to undermine or complement Australia's workforce.

Myth Check

Labour Market Integration

Comparing labour market outcomes between Australian-born and overseas-born residents reveals a remarkably high degree of integration. Contrary to the common belief that migrants take jobs or suppress local wages, the differences between the two groups are minimal.

Unemployment rates differ by only 0.1 percentage points (4.7% vs 4.8%), while median weekly earnings remain broadly comparable across both populations. Such small gaps suggest that migrant workers participate in the labour market under similar conditions to Australian-born Australians rather than displacing them.

These findings indicate that migration complements Australia's workforce and helps address labour shortages without producing meaningful evidence of widespread wage suppression or job displacement.

Data Source: ABS Permanent Migrants in Australia  ·  Additional Reference: ABS Characteristics of Recent Migrants (Labour Outcomes)
06

Conclusion

Migration has become an essential component of Australia's economic and demographic development. The evidence presented throughout this dashboard shows that migrants contribute to population growth, support industries facing labour shortages, strengthen high-value sectors, and participate in the labour market at outcomes comparable to Australian-born residents. While challenges surrounding growth and infrastructure remain important policy considerations, the data suggests that migration functions primarily as a driver of workforce sustainability, productivity, and long-term economic prosperity.
