01

The Big Picture

Australia's economy has been strongly shaped by immigration. Migrants bring skills, labour, and cultural diversity into the workforce. This visualisation explores how immigration contributes to economic growth by showing where migrants settle, which industries they support, and how they help address labour demand. Rather than viewing immigration only as population growth, this project presents it as an important force behind Australia's workforce and long-term economic development.

Population Growth

Australia's Overseas-Born Population Continues to Rise

Australia's population growth has been closely tied to net overseas migration rather than natural increase alone. Migration became the largest contributor to total population growth, particularly after the reopening of international borders following COVID-19.

Settlement Patterns

Where Migrants Settle in Australia

Migrants are not evenly distributed across Australia. States such as Western Australia, Victoria, and New South Wales consistently show higher overseas-born population shares, reflecting their stronger links to jobs, universities, and urban economic centres. The slider shows how migrant settlement has grown from 2001 to 2021.

Country of Origin

Top Migrant-Origin Countries in Australia

Australia's migrant population comes from a wide range of countries, reflecting the nation's multicultural identity and global migration links. Countries such as England, India, China, and New Zealand represent some of the largest overseas-born communities, contributing significantly to Australia's workforce, education sector, and economic growth.

02

Migrants Power the Workforce

Behind Australia's economic growth is a workforce quietly holding industries together. Migrants make up a significant share of workers across nearly every sector — from hospitals and construction sites to tech offices and restaurant kitchens. But their contribution isn't spread evenly. Some industries lean heavily on overseas-born workers to fill roles that not enough Australians are taking up. This section maps where migrants are working across Australia — by industry and by state — revealing the invisible backbone of the Australian labour market.

Industry Breakdown

Migrant Workers Support Key Australian Industries

Certain industries rely disproportionately on overseas-born workers. Healthcare, construction, professional services, and accommodation sectors all show elevated shares of migrant employees, many of whom fill critical shortages in specialised roles.

Occupational Roles

Top Occupations Filled by Skilled Migrants

Skilled migrants play an important role in filling workforce shortages across Australia's essential industries. Healthcare, construction, hospitality, and technology-related occupations consistently rely on migrant workers to meet growing labour demand and support economic productivity.

While migrants fill immediate workforce gaps across many sectors, a more pressing question is whether migration is being strategically directed to address Australia's critical skill shortages. The following section examines how skilled migration pathways align — or misalign — with the occupational needs projected for Australia's future economy.

03

Filling Australia's Skill Gaps

Australia's economy is increasingly dependent on targeted skilled migration to address chronic shortages in key professions. Whether it is nurses in regional hospitals, engineers in mining, or software developers in capital cities, the evidence points to a labour market that cannot sustain growth without international talent. This section examines the distribution of skill gaps and the role migrants play in closing them.

Skills Analysis

Industries with the Highest Migrant Skill Reliance

Certain high-skill sectors — including information technology, medical specialties, and advanced manufacturing — show a particularly strong dependence on overseas-qualified workers, highlighting structural gaps in Australia's domestic training pipeline.

Economic Contribution

How Skilled Migrants Drive Economic Output

Skilled migrants contribute disproportionately to Australia's GDP relative to their population share. By taking roles in high-value sectors, they not only fill vacancies but actively expand Australia's productive capacity and international competitiveness.

Beyond targeted skills, the overall volume of immigration operates as a macro-level dynamic that powers economic expansion. The final section examines how these aggregate flows closely correlate with GDP growth and the broader value of industrial output.

04

Economic Contribution

Immigration is not just a demographic driver; it is a major economic engine. By infusing the labour market with diverse talents, bridging vital industry vacancies, and boosting consumer demand, migrants directly stimulate economic output. This section visualises the tight coupling between migration trends and GDP fluctuations, alongside a detailed breakdown of how key industry sectors lean on and prosper from migrant labour to fuel Australia's economic future.

GDP vs Migration

Migration and GDP Growth Through Economic Shocks

This indexed comparison shows the clear synchronisation between Net Overseas Migration and GDP growth in Australia. As external shocks like COVID-19 restricted travel, both migration and GDP plunged, while subsequent rebounds underscore how migration remains central to economic vitality.
